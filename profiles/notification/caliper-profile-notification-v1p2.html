Abstract

This document extends Caliper 1.2 by defining a Notification Profile that describes the preparation, sending, and handling of notices of important information to users.

Overview

Design Goals and Rationale

The Notification Profile addresses a gap in learning analytics by providing a standardized way to track and analyze the volume, timing, and characteristics of notifications sent to students across educational technology systems. The primary design goals include:

  • Intervention Impact Measurement: Enable researchers and educators to quantify how notification saturation affects the effectiveness of educational interventions and student engagement.
  • Cross-System Notification Tracking: Provide a unified framework for tracking notifications across multiple educational platforms (LMS, early alert systems, mobile apps, email systems) to understand the complete notification landscape students experience.
  • Contamination Bias Mitigation: Support more accurate measurement of intervention effectiveness by accounting for confounding notifications that may influence control groups in educational research studies.
  • Student Experience Optimization: Student Experience Optimization: Enable institutions to analyze and optimize their notification strategies to reduce student overwhelm while maintaining effective communication.

Current educational technology ecosystems generate substantial volumes of notifications to students through various channels including email, push notifications, LMS announcements, and in-app messaging. Research indicates that students receive overwhelming amounts of institutional communication, with email open rates as low as 50% in some contexts, suggesting notification fatigue.

The existing Caliper Message Event, designed primarily for forum-based interactions between persons, does not adequately capture system-generated notifications or the broader spectrum of institutional communications. This limitation creates a significant blind spot in learning analytics, particularly for:

  • Intervention Research: Provide a unified framework for tracking notifications across multiple educational platforms (LMS, early alert systems, mobile apps, email systems) to understand the complete notification landscape students experience.
  • Student Success Initiatives: Institutions lack visibility into how their various notification systems collectively impact student engagement and success.
  • Platform Coordination: Educational technology vendors cannot assess how their notifications interact with the broader ecosystem of student communications.

The Notification Profile fills this gap by extending Caliper's capabilities to capture system-generated notifications while maintaining privacy and avoiding surveillance of private interpersonal communications.

User Stories

The Notification Profile supports the following user stories:

Educational Researcher
"As an educational researcher conducting an A/B test of study reminder interventions, I need to track all notifications sent to students in both treatment and control groups so I can account for contamination bias and accurately measure my intervention's effectiveness."
Student Success Analyst
"As a student success analyst, I need to understand the total volume and timing of notifications students receive across all institutional systems so I can identify potential notification fatigue and optimize our communication strategy."
Institutional Adminstrator
"As an institutional administrator, I need visibility into notification patterns across different student populations and courses so I can develop evidence-based policies for student communication."
Learning Analytics Platform
"As a learning analytics platform, I need to receive standardized notification event data from various educational tools so I can provide comprehensive insights about factors affecting student engagement."
Educational Technology Vendor
"As an EdTech vendor, I need to log my system's notification events in a standardized format so institutions can understand how my notifications fit into their broader student communication ecosystem."
